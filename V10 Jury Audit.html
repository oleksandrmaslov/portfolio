Frame by frame

01 Title — framed universe Polish

What worksThe corner-reticle frame, pointer shields, and staggered rise+blur entrance are jury-grade details. The field guide that dismisses on genuine interaction (and stays dismissed per-session) shows UX maturity.

What a juror flagsThe ASCII M.O. wordmark read as static — your instinct was right. The animation loop was running, but the brightness math saturated: letter cells clamped at full density (@) every frame, and background noise never crossed a character threshold. At v10's smaller font size the residual shimmer became invisible. Fixed this pass — letters now breathe across the density ramp with a slow luminance sweep, and the cyan highlight rides the sweep crest.

Still openThe first five seconds carry ~40% of a jury score. Consider a one-time boot sequence (frame snap → universe ignition → wordmark resolve) so the first impression is choreographed, not simultaneous.

02 Intro — editorial "who" Keep

What works"I build small, careful objects — and the firmware that runs them" is the best line on the site. Specific, human, memorable. The signed/field blocks ground it.

What a juror flagsThis is the screen that earns a calm beat — but the meta rows (NOW / OPEN TO / WRITE) compete with the headline. Consider letting the headline own the screen and revealing the meta rows only on scroll-out. Small restraint, big perceived confidence.

03 Work reel — scroll → pan Polish

What worksSnap-by-easing dwell, parallax bg at 0.35×, the reel position published to the WebGL field every frame, sound-score hooks on stop changes — this is the most award-level engineering on the page. The "Open the universe" gate as slot N+1 is a great close.

What a juror flagsThe cards are caption bars — deliberately low so the universe stays hero, but a juror skimming in 60 seconds sees four text strips. This is where the Wafer-quality renders pay off most: each locked card should surface its model or hero render in the field behind it. Your GLB-per-project plan is exactly right; the reel is the place it converts to score.

What worksScroll-to-lock with the tightening reticle, the armed state flipping the key to "DIVE → 0x00", the ignition handoff to the board page — narrative and mechanics are fused. This is the section that wins Creativity points.

What a juror flags260vh of scroll for one beat is fine ONLY if the lock progression feels continuous — make sure the percentage readout and reticle tighten visibly through the whole range, not just near the end.

The gap to Lusion / Immersive Garden

  • 02

  • 03
    A designed mobile experience. Not a squeezed desktop — a sibling. The universe likely becomes a touch-drift field; the reel becomes native horizontal swipe. Jurors absolutely score mobile, and this is currently the largest missing surface. You picked "design it with me" — that's our next major work block.
  • 04
    Production build. In-browser Babel + development React is fine for prototyping but disqualifying for submission — jurors open devtools, and first-load time is scored. Precompile, minify, self-host fonts, lazy-load the universe.
  • 05
    The sound invitation. score.js hooks exist; the opt-in moment needs design. One elegant beat on the title screen — a keycap, not a modal. Done well, opt-in audio is a differentiator jurors mention by name.
  • 06
    Moments of calm. The instrument chrome is the identity, but Lusion's pages breathe — big quiet frames between dense ones. Right now every screen runs at the same density. The intro is the natural place to let one statement own the viewport.
