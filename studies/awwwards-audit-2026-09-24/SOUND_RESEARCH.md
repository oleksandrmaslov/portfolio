# Sound research · 25 September 2026

The first sketch was rejected: the owner heard straight noise and preferred the
old version. The previous live sound and its visual hooks are restored. Earlier
navigation, portfolio identification, origin copy and video-upload fixes remain.
The rejected source/assets are archived under `sound/rejected-first-pass/`,
outside deployment. No replacement sound is approved or implemented by this
research pass.

## Brief and the mistake

The audience is creative developers and collaborators. The universe is made by
Oleksandr: the teal particles are his presence, and 0x00 is their origin. The
requested sound has a very quiet continuous atmosphere, some of THE FINALS and
Valorant's character, and a welcoming mood. It must not feel scary, abandoned,
oppressive or generically cosmic. The universe should hold visual attention.

The failed sketch made filtered random noise the main continuous layer and
placed subdued tonal material under it. It had no developed musical phrase and
no personally meaningful recorded source. Lowering the number of audio nodes
did not give it an identity. Describing it as warm or tactile did not establish
those qualities through listening. The architecture and the creative result
must be evaluated separately.

## What the creators actually say

**THE FINALS: emotion and meaning precede the effect.** In Embark's audio
interview, the team distinguishes uplifting in-game music from the harsher
trailer treatment (27:06). For rewarding cues they explain timing, frequency
placement and association (04:13–06:04); their material choices connect to the
game-show setting, including a recorded reception bell. These are design
principles, not a reason to add casino sounds to this portfolio.
[Official podcast](https://open.spotify.com/episode/5ciiOdwOaDP2s15jF8FUgd),
[published transcript used for the timestamps](https://www.thefinals.wiki/wiki/Meet_The_Makers%CB%90_Audio).

**THE FINALS: its musical identity is deliberately composed.** The first
official album credits Sailor & I; the second credits Carl Strandberg, with
additional collaborators on selected tracks. The S2 release explicitly uses
electro/chiptune/synthwave tags. A digital visual world does not require an
unpitched wash.
[S1 album and credits](https://embarkstudios.bandcamp.com/album/the-finals-original-soundtrack),
[S2 album and credits](https://embarkstudios.bandcamp.com/album/the-finals-cns-lha).

**Valorant: music and designed effects have separate jobs.** Riot describes
working with Lick and Jesse Harlin on music, recording sources including toys
and wrapping paper for effects, and revising audio through playtests. Ryan
Harlin's own portfolio identifies his menu contributions for Episodes 3–7 and
links examples. Its sound is not evidence that a single procedural synth should
do the music, atmosphere and every interaction.
[Riot audio-team account](https://playvalorant.com/en-us/news/dev/end-of-year-audio-discipline/),
[Ryan Harlin's portfolio](https://ryanharlin.com/).

**Sound should explain an event.** Riot's sound-design introduction frames
effects around communicating events, responding to decisions and supporting
immersion. For this page, opening a project is an event; every pointer movement
is not automatically an event worth voicing.
[Riot: Sound Design](https://www.riotgames.com/en/artedu/sound-design).

The findings above are from creator statements, published credits and the
linked transcript. The media links below were verified, but no acoustic
audition or listening-quality claim is made here. These are candidates to
compare, not tracks already judged to match the owner's taste.

## Proposed direction, subject to that listening comparison

Use a small, composed electronic score with audible harmony and a recognizable
short phrase. Keep it quiet and give the phrase space between appearances.
Gentle musical motion can make the place feel inhabited without crowd noise,
room hiss, a heartbeat, or a threatening low drone. This is an interpretation
of the brief, not a finding that a particular chord or tempo is already right.

Make the phrase belong to **0x00**. The universe contains incomplete glimpses
of it; the origin reveal assembles its first clear statement. Repetition of the
same sound identity would express the same author being present in every
project. Avoid assigning unrelated random notes to twelve project addresses.
The identity must work when heard without the explanatory paragraph.

Give the UI a restrained, related set of edited sounds. A project opening needs
a definite beginning, body and finish. A focus cue should be lighter and rare;
scrolling need not add a layer. Real sounds from an actual project may supply
personal texture, but a synthetic instrument can also work. Source provenance
alone does not make a sound pleasant, and the existing archive has not yet been
auditioned for usable clean material.

The initial sound comparison should separate atmosphere, one interaction and
the origin phrase. Evaluate their character independently, then their mix
together at comparable perceived levels. Keep the next sketch out of the live
portfolio until the owner has heard it. This sequencing follows the owner's
request to research first after rejecting the previous pass.

## Runtime decisions come after the sound

The original source audit still applies: many continuous voices, repeating
scroll notes, a separate wind output and overlapping accents. It identifies
work worth reducing, not the source of musical quality. Once the sound is
chosen, render expensive processing into assets and keep playback, modest
crossfades and bounded interaction cues in the browser. Check transfer size,
decoded memory, source cleanup and frame/audio-thread behavior separately.
Neither oscillator counts nor unit tests demonstrate perceived quality or
prove an FPS improvement.

Menu presentation and an arranged universe mode remain later work. No new
visual redesign is bundled into this research pass.
