Wafer.

A 36-key wireless split keyboard, 4–8 mm depending where you measure.

Live 3D model of the keyboard, built from its CAD parts. Photographs appear where I have them.

Drag to turn it. Scroll to take it places.

Drag to turn it, or focus it and use the left and right arrow keys; Home puts it back. Type on your own keyboard to press its keys. Esc or Tab lets go.

01 / 08 · Desk

Two halves.

Thirty-six keys in two halves you can place shoulder-width apart. Wireless, with nothing between them.

Flat on purpose: a thin split, not a contoured one. Numbers and symbols live on layers.

Click or Tab to it, then type on your keyboard. Esc lets go.Tap a key. The model types with you.

03 / 08 · Screens

Screens.

Two memory-in-pixel panels, one per half. No backlight: they read by room light.

On the prototype, the right screen shows what’s playing, the left the clock and layer. Here the page drives both: narration, typing, taps, volume. That part is a page trick.

Build record

Thinness is a stack budget.

Switches, board, battery, screens, magnets and an aluminium wall compete for the same few millimetres.

More than thirty printed cases came before the case went to CNC. That run was an iteration: it exposed tolerance problems, and where to add material back.

The case was ordered, not milled by me.

The battery reporting for the power chip lives in my own ZMK fork, not upstream.

The board began from Mikoto’s MCU schematic; I rebuilt it twice.

Built, modelled, unproven.
